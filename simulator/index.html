How to read it. A good forecast tracks the black line without lagging behind spikes or sitting above it in calm weeks. Error = QLIKE, the standard loss for volatility forecasts (0 = perfect); "average miss" is the typical gap in volatility points.

Quoting a 1-day ATM straddle with each model's volatility

Each day an option desk quotes an at-the-money straddle using the model's forecast. Line = money mispriced so far (₹): the gap between the quote and the fair price at the volatility that actually happened. Lower is better.

Average pricing error per day
Why it matters. A straddle pays the size of the move, so its fair price is about 0.8 × volatility × notional. A forecast that is too low gives premium away to buyers; one that is too high loses trades to better-priced dealers. Every rupee of forecast error becomes a rupee of mispricing. A zero-cost, frictionless 1-day option is a teaching simplification; real NSE stock options are monthly and trade around implied volatility.

How much margin to hold on a futures position

Margin = multiplier × forecast volatility × position. A breach is a day when the intraday loss was bigger than the margin held (long position).

Breaches, this stock
Trade-off. All models breach more often than a normal distribution implies, because daily moves have fat tails; exchanges add a buffer for this. A forecast that runs high (classic HAR-RV) ties up more capital; one that runs low breaches more often than the target rate. The best model hits the target with the least capital. Grey bars = actual daily loss; coloured line = margin held by the selected model.

Volatility targeting: keep each position's daily risk at a budget

Position size = risk budget ÷ forecast volatility. Line = realized daily risk over the past 20 days; dashed = budget.

Realized risk vs budget, this stock
Why it matters. Portfolio managers and CTAs size positions by forecast volatility so that no single name dominates the risk. If the forecast is too high, risk runs below budget and capital sits idle; if too low, risk overshoots. A stable line near the budget is the goal.

Where the deep-learning model earns its keep

Each dot is a stock: QLIKE gain of HAR-RV-LSTM over log-HAR (%), against liquidity rank (1 = most liquid). Click a dot to open that stock.

Gain over log-HAR, least liquid third
Reading. In the most liquid stocks (Reliance, SBI, Infosys…) the simple log-HAR model is already as good. As liquidity falls, realized volatility gets noisier and the LSTM's gain grows. Use the simple model where markets are deep; the LSTM pays off across a broad, mixed-liquidity book.
